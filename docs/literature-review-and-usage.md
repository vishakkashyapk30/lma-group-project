## 1. Purpose and how this answers the TA's call for agentic-engineering novelty

The TA's feedback asked for novelty in agentic engineering, with baselines and ablations, not a new model. This review does three things for that call.

1. **It places each design choice against published work**, so the report can say what is borrowed, what is adapted and what is new. The honest summary is that most individual ideas exist: routing among LLMs, adaptive retrieval, sufficiency checking, claim-level verification, debate, dependency-tracked memory, prompt-injection architecture. What does not exist, as far as this search found, is the combination for one workflow: declarative evidence contracts over typed legal stores with per-factor sufficiency checks and budgets, page-level ingestion routing with gap-driven re-reading, a context-isolated prosecution critic scored against recorded objections, and dependency-tracked redrafting behind code-enforced approval gates. That is a claim about engineering of a system, and it has to be backed by ablations rather than asserted.
2. **It turns every adopted idea into a test that needs no manual labels** (Section 4), so each novelty claim can fail. Examples: a sampling-only critic at equal tokens against Abhiyojak; a self-critique-only arm against tool-grounded repair; oracle regret for the ingestion ladder; stopped-too-early and over-search rates for Sutradhar; a null-agent check on every metric.
3. **It says plainly what does not transfer.** The routing papers (including the team-named AgentRouter) route among LLMs or answering agents; our stores are not agents, and our action space is small. IntrAgent is built for STEM papers with expert-written questions. Debate papers mostly find voting explains the gains. Many 2026 items are preprints. Section 4 and Section 6 record these limits.

**Findings that most change the plan**
- Stopping and sufficiency are the most direct match to Sutradhar (Sufficient Context, IntrAgent, AutoSearch). Rule checks plus an LLM rater are worth an ablation; training a stopper is not.
- Self-correction without outside evidence is unreliable (Cannot Self-Correct, Small Models Need Strong Verifiers). Vakeel's NLI-and-tool repair is the right design, but it must be compared with a self-critique arm.
- Debate is no better than sampling at equal cost in several studies (Debate or Vote, Should We Be Going MAD). Abhiyojak must beat a sampling-only critic, and isolation should be ablated (Talk Isn't Always Cheap).
- Prompt injection is an architecture problem (Design Patterns, CaMeL). Our typed hand-off and approval gates fit; font-based hidden text (Invisible Prompts) is a new threat for a PDF-first system.
- Agent evaluation needs cost and reliability, not one accuracy number (AI Agents That Matter, tau-bench, Agent Reliability, Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks).
- Legal AI work on India is mostly outcome prediction (IBPS, NyayaRAG, PredEx); we position as preparation with checked citations, the setting where legal RAG tools have been shown to hallucinate (Hallucination-Free?).

## 2. Method

**Scope.** Fourteen themes, listed in Section 3. Search targeted venues from 2023 to 2026: ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS, ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, COLM, AAAI, IJCAI, SIGIR, ICTIR, USENIX, OSDI, MLSys, TMLR, TACL, ICAIL, plus arXiv for items without a venue. Today is 21 October 2026.

**How papers were checked.** Two earlier passes and this one:
1. A sweep per theme found candidates and recorded an official link and venue for each.
2. A second pass verified 33 papers individually against official pages (proceedings or conference sites, ACL Anthology, arXiv).
3. In this run I re-fetched the official page for 25 more papers, including the two the team named (IntrAgent: ACL 2026 Long Papers, pp. 674 to 715; AgentRouter: ACL 2026 Long Papers, pp. 788 to 809, both confirmed on the ACL Anthology), and for the ones whose venue looked doubtful. Several venue claims were corrected: olmOCR and AutoGen show no venue on arXiv (an earlier note of "ICML 2025 workshop" for olmOCR and the "COLM 2024" listing for AutoGen are unconfirmed and are labelled as such); "When Citations Mislead?" states ICAIL 2026 in its arXiv comments.

**Check column in the tables.** A = verified by the second pass on an official page; M = fetched and confirmed in this run (for preprints this means the arXiv page was read and shows no venue); A+M = both; S = taken from the first sweep, which recorded an official link, but the venue and authors were not re-fetched. Treat S entries as probably right and re-check the venue before putting them in a final report.

**Tier column.** top-venue = main or Findings track of a major conference or a TMLR/TACL journal article, as stated on the official page; workshop-or-other = workshop paper, short or companion paper, or a journal outside the core list; preprint = arXiv only.

Most papers are 3 or below.

**Limits of this review.** Contribution lines come from abstracts and the earlier summaries, not from reading each paper in full. Reported numbers (for example "attack success under 2 percent") are the papers' own results on their own benchmarks and need not hold for Qwen3-4B or Indian bail documents. I did not check the code or data availability of most benchmarks; items that depend on a dataset say so.

## 5. Changes to the research questions, baselines and ablations

The two research questions stay as they are. What changes is how sharply each is tested, so that gains cannot be explained by cheaper alternatives (more samples, simpler prompts, a free planner).

**RQ1 (orchestration).** Keep the wording. Add three sub-hypotheses, each answered by an ablation that already exists or is cheap:
- **H1d (stopping).** Rule-based sufficiency plus an LLM sufficiency rater stops sooner and no later than rules alone, at equal G1 recall (R1, ablation A11b).
- **H1e (sparring vs sampling).** Abhiyojak's objection recall (G4) is higher than a sampling-only critic given the same tokens (A1, arm A8b), and isolation matters (A2, arm A8c). Either null result is reported.
- **H1f (cost frontier).** The full system dominates B4 and A7 on the quality-cost frontier across budgets, not only at the default budget (R4).

**RQ2 (ingestion).** Keep the wording and H2. Add the oracle-regret view of the ladder (U2), noise-type breakdown (U4) and one more baseline, P7 (image-only vision pipeline, U5), plus the bundle-splitting baseline (U3).

## 6. Considered but not adopted

Reasons are specific to our constraints: open-weight models at run time, 11 GB and 8 GB GPUs, no manual annotation, 8 weeks, and the need for an auditable legal record.

| Idea (papers) | Why not |
|---|---|
| Trained routers, searchers and stoppers: Router-R1, Search-R1, AutoSearch, Stop-RAG, MasRouter, RouterDC | Most need many rollouts or labelled routing data and GPU time we do not have. We take their loop shape and cost-aware reward as evaluation ideas (R1, R4) and keep rules and contracts as the policy. |
| Automated workflow and agent search: AFlow, ADAS, MaAS, GPTSwarm, MASS, SelfOrg | Search costs thousands of LLM calls and returns workflows that are hard to audit. A legal-aid tool needs a fixed, explainable pipeline. Only MaAS's difficulty-adaptive effort is borrowed (R7). |
| LLM-managed memory: MemGPT, Mem0, A-Mem | An LLM deciding what to forget conflicts with an event-sourced, auditable case record. Mem0's venue claim could not be confirmed. CoALA is used for vocabulary only (M3). |
| Entity-graph RAG built by LLMs: GraphRAG, LightRAG, G-Retriever | Heavy indexing cost, and built for corpus-wide summaries or textual graphs; our graph comes from extracted citations. When to Use Graphs in RAG finds graph RAG often loses to plain RAG, which is a reason to test PPR as a small ablation only (S2). |
| Vision-first document retrieval as the main path: ColPali, VisRAG | Needs a vision model on every page; too costly on small GPUs. Kept as a baseline (P7). |
| Training-based injection defences: StruQ, SecAlign | Need fine-tuning with special tokens or preference training; we use prompt and architecture defences (SF1 to SF3). |
| Detector-style injection defences: MELON, IPIGuard, Task Shield | Not needed in addition to typed hand-offs and code approval gates; MELON re-runs each trajectory, doubling cost. Worth citing in a related-work paragraph. |
| CaMeL's full interpreter with capabilities | Heavy to build in 8 weeks; we adopt the plan-then-execute and dual-LLM ideas only (SF1). |
| Trained debaters and critics: ACC-Collab, persuasion-optimised debate | Training cost, and our critic's score is the recorded prosecution objection, not persuasion. |
| Simulated courts: AgentCourt, LegalSim | Different task (civil trial simulation, procedure exploitation). Related work only (A3). |
| Outcome and judgment prediction: IBPS predictors, NyayaRAG, PredEx | The system refuses outcome prediction by design (plan 1.3). We cite them for positioning and for existing data (L1, L2). |
| Engine-level serving changes: KVFlow, Autellix, CacheBlend, Continuum | Need a modified serving engine; we only reuse their scheduling and caching ideas at the application level (B2, B3). |
| Learning-to-rank scheduling by predicted output length | Urgency order must be legally explainable and fixed (plan 11), not driven by a length predictor. |
| Full conformal factuality guarantees | Need labelled calibration data. We would use constructed negatives only (V4) and say the guarantee is weaker. |
| Collaborative agents with real users: Collaborative Gym, CollabLLM's user study | No human subjects available in the project (H3). |
| General agent suites: AgentBench, AutoGen, tau-bench's retail and airline tasks | Different domains. tau-bench's pass^k metric is adopted; the tasks are not. |
| Other-jurisdiction legal benchmarks: LegalBench, LawBench, LegalAgentBench, CLERC | US or Chinese law. Their methods (process-level scoring, citation-retrieval framing) inform metrics only. |
| Failure attribution by agent and step (Who&When) | Best methods reach 14 percent step-level accuracy; MAST-style group tagging is enough for us (R6). |
