\section{Preliminary Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{What this section is.} The results so far, from small development runs. Every number comes from a table written by the scoring pipeline (\texttt{python -m vakeel\_sahayak.eval.scoring score}, then \texttt{report}, which writes the fragments in \texttt{docs/results/dev-sweep/tables/}) or from \texttt{docs/report/tools/make\_result\_tables.py}, which reads the per-case scores of each model family. Where the text quotes a number it quotes the table; every cell carries the number of cases behind it, because most dev orders have no scorable gold for most metrics. The project is a work in progress; the results are a first look, not a ranking.

\paragraph{What ran.} Three sets of runs exist, and a table compares arms only inside one set (Table~\ref{tab:models}). (1) \emph{Superseded dev runs} (tag \texttt{dev-sweep-v1}, commit \texttt{efd0ce4}): \texttt{full} (two runs), \texttt{b0}, \texttt{b1}, \texttt{b4} and \texttt{b6} on all 50 dev cases, OpenAI \texttt{gpt-6-luna} (hosted) behind the pseudonymisation boundary. They are kept as the only 50-case baseline run; the code has changed since. (2) \emph{OpenAI block on the current tag} (\texttt{dev-sweep-v3}, commit \texttt{7ad6ed4}): \texttt{full}, \texttt{b0} to \texttt{b3} on 38 cases, \texttt{b4} and the ablations A1, A2, A3, A6, A7, A8 and A9 on up to 30, and B5, S1, S2, S3, S3b, S4, S5, S6, S8, S9, S10, S11, S12, S15 and S18 on 10. The first 25 cases are the first 25 of the dev file; the rest are the dev cases whose gold the factor and objection metrics can score, chosen from the gold labels alone and the same for every arm. (3) \emph{Gemini blocks} (hosted, free tier, public court text only): \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash-lite} with \texttt{full}, \texttt{b0} to \texttt{b3} and B0L (on \texttt{gemini-3.6-flash}) at 10 cases and A1 at 5; and \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} with \texttt{full}, A2 and A8 at 5. Run since that list was written (our block, below): A4, A5, S11b, S11c, S12b and S15b on \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}, and A10, S7, S17 and P5 to P8 offline on the stand-in model. Still not run on any model: S13; the retrieval and scheduling variants S4, S5, S14 and S16 were measured without a model in the offline studies below. A case that the privacy boundary refused for a hosted call (eight of the first 25) fell back to a local model that was not running; the scorer counts those as empty briefs, so every paired comparison below uses only the cases both arms answered without error. Scoring is on the dev slice, agreed gold, MiniCheck for the unsupported-fact rate.

\paragraph{Main comparison.} Tables~\ref{tab:table1a_v1} and~\ref{tab:table1b_v1} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) compare Full with the baselines on factor recall, lenient precision and F1 (Table~\ref{tab:table1a_v1}) and on the unsupported-fact rate, objection recall, answerable coverage, coverage, tokens and cost (Table~\ref{tab:table1b_v1}); recall@10 of court-cited precedents is undefined on dev (Section~\ref{sec:eval}) and is measured in the offline studies below. On factor recall Full scores 0.25 (95\% interval 0.00 to 0.63, $n=8$), below B0 (0.50), B1 and B4 (0.63 each) and B6 (0.88); every interval overlaps Full's, so none of the gaps is resolved. Its unsupported-fact rate (0.87, $n=50$) is above B0 (0.83), B1 (0.76) and B4 (0.81) and below B6 (0.98). Full is clearly ahead of B0, B1 and B6 on objection recall (0.69 against 0.00, $n=20$) and behind B4 (0.93). None of the six registered recall comparisons survives the Holm correction (the smallest $p$ is 0.25), and the comparisons with B0L, B2 and B3 were not run on this code, so claim C1 is incomplete, not supported.

\paragraph{Results on the current code (OpenAI \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, hosted).} \gentable{fig_openai} \gentable{forest_openai} \gentable{cost_openai} This block (tag \texttt{dev-sweep-v3}) has Full and B0 to B3 on 38 dev orders, B4 and the ablations on up to 30, and the variants on 10; every comparison is paired with Full on the orders both arms answered, and a cell counts only the orders where the metric is defined. \emph{Against the baselines} (appendix tables): B0 to B3 argue none of the prosecution's objections (objection recall 0.00 against Full's 0.74 to 0.79 on 14 to 17 orders), while B4, which queries every store, is higher than Full (0.90 against 0.75, 20 orders); answerable-factor coverage is 0.64 for B4 against 0.42 for Full (12 orders) and near zero for B0 to B3. Full's unsupported-fact rate is no better than the baselines' (0.87 to 0.89 against 0.82 to 0.87), and its factor recall (0.17 to 0.25) is below B0, B1 and B4 (0.50 to 0.75) on the 4 to 8 orders where both are defined, with intervals that overlap, so factor recall is unresolved. Full has no fabricated citation on 31 orders. Full costs 95,466 tokens and \$0.0100 a case against 2,907 tokens and \$0.0003 for B0 and 126,301 tokens and \$0.0133 for B4. \emph{Against the ablations}, three effects have intervals that exclude zero on 28 to 30 orders. Removing the sparring agent (A8) lowers objection recall by 0.75 (95\% interval $-0.94$ to $-0.53$) and changes nothing else. Removing check and repair (A2) lowers answerable coverage by 0.42 ($-0.67$ to $-0.17$) and citation precision by 0.39 ($-0.50$ to $-0.29$); coverage falls to zero by construction, because it is defined through the check verdicts. Removing both (A9, the $2\times2$ arm) adds the two effects without any visible interaction. Removing the dossier (A1) lowers the unsupported-fact rate by 0.10 ($-0.18$ to $-0.02$) and citation precision by 0.20 ($-0.36$ to $-0.03$): on this metric the simpler arm is better than Full. Removing the tools (A3) lowers citation precision by 0.08 ($-0.14$ to $-0.02$), and a fixed router (A6) is within noise (objection recall $+0.07$, interval $0.00$ to $+0.20$). A free planner (A7, 18 orders so far) and the learned router baseline B5 lower coverage by 0.54 and 0.63. The claim that the full harness beats every baseline is therefore not supported on these runs: it is clearly better than the simple baselines on objection recall and answerable coverage, it is not better than the query-every-store baseline B4 on those, and it is not better than any baseline on the unsupported-fact rate.

\paragraph{Results on the Gemini family (hosted, free tier).} \gentable{fig_gemini} \gentable{forest_gemini} On \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash-lite}, with its own Full on 5 to 8 shared cases, Full's objection recall is 0.25 against 0.00 for B0 to B3 ($n=4$; the interval of the difference, $-0.75$ to $0.00$ for the baselines, touches zero), its unsupported-fact rate is 0.94 against 0.86 to 1.00, and its factor recall is 0.00 on two cases, so this family does not separate Full from the baselines at its sample size. B3's coverage is 0.67 against Full's 0.92 on six cases, and the one comparison on citation precision (Full 1.00 against B1's 0.00, $n=2$) is too small to read. This family has its own Full and is not compared with the OpenAI numbers; a separate \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} block (block B), with its own Full and the A4, A5 and S-variant arms, is reported next.

\paragraph{Gemini block B and the offline store knock-outs (our runs).} \gentable{ablate_shravani_gem} On \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}, with its own Full on two paired dev cases, we ran the ablations A4 (plain retrieval) and A5 (base-4B extractor) and the variants S11b (debate), S11c (vote), S12b (single agent) and S15b (free-then-parse). No comparison is resolved: every 95\% interval includes zero at $n=2$. The multi-agent and single-agent drafting changes sit at or just below Full on coverage (S11b and S15b $-0.20$, interval $-0.40$ to $0.00$; S11c and S12b $-0.10$), plain retrieval (A4) is just above ($+0.10$), the unsupported-fact rate moves by at most $0.04$, and objection recall, answerable coverage and factor recall have no agreed gold on these two cases. The block stopped when the free-tier daily quota was spent, so this is a directional first look only. \gentable{ablate_shravani_off} The offline store knock-outs (A10, ten dev cases, offline stand-in model) remove one of the seven stores at a time. On the stand-in, removing any store empties the brief, so all seven knock-outs score identically: against Full, objection recall falls by $1.00$ (interval $-1.00$ to $-1.00$), coverage by $0.76$ ($-0.96$ to $-0.56$) and factor recall by $0.50$ ($-1.00$ to $0.00$). This shows the pipeline depends on every store but, because the knock-outs are indistinguishable, it measures the plumbing and the stand-in's brittleness, not how a hosted model weighs each store. The intake arms S7 and P5 to P8 (test set T2) and the injection-defence arm S17 (T7) were run offline but their ingestion and attack-success metrics are not yet wired to the scorer, so they are run, not scored.

\paragraph{Superseded 50-case baseline run.} \gentable{forest_v1} \gentable{cost_v1} The 50-case run shows what the full harness adds in cost and in sparring: Full is ahead of B0, B1 and B6 on objection recall (0.69 against 0.00) and on answerable coverage (0.50 against 0.00), costs about 26 times B0's tokens, and is behind B6 on factor recall (0.25 against 0.88 on 8 cases, interval $-1.00$ to $-0.13$ for the difference, $p=0.125$ uncorrected). We read this as a first result: the harness is built for the objections and the checked citations (Full is the only arm that regularly cites authorities, with a citation precision of 0.79 and a fabricated-citation rate of 0.00, both on 74 orders), and the factor-recall comparison needs a larger scorable set than these 8 orders.

\paragraph{What each baseline shows.} B0 (single pass) isolates the effect of having no retrieval and no agents: B0 scores below Full on factor recall only at $n=8$ and ties it on unsupported facts (0.83 against 0.87); it cannot argue the objections a prosecutor would raise (objection recall 0.00). B1 and B3 (one store, with or without naive chunking) isolate the effect of several typed stores against one: one store gives higher factor recall than Full (0.63 against 0.25, not resolved) and a lower unsupported-fact rate (0.76 against 0.87, paired difference $+0.095$, interval $+0.03$ to $+0.16$, not rejected after Holm), also with no objection recall. B2 (whole-order embeddings) isolates retrieval granularity: B2 and B3 have run on the current code only (Block A, Gemini 10 cases and OpenAI 25 to 38 cases), not on the 50-case run. B4 (every store, fused) isolates an agent choosing against querying everything, and its token cost against Full: B4 reaches higher objection recall than Full (0.93 against 0.69; the paired difference for Full is $-0.24$, interval $-0.45$ to $-0.04$) and the same factor recall (0.63 against 0.25, not resolved), at a higher cost (131,239 tokens a case against 101,860 for Full; \$0.0138 against \$0.0107). Its answerable-factor coverage (0.75) is also higher than Full's (0.50, $n=20$), while its overall coverage is lower (0.45 against 0.83): fusing every store finds more of what the gold needs but leaves more orders with unanswered factors. B6 (expert rule list) gives the no-model floor and is scored on precision, unsupported-fact rate and the Rights checks only: its factor recall is the highest of all arms (0.88, $n=8$) and its unsupported-fact rate the worst (0.98): a fixed text of rules matches the factors but supports none of its sentences from the facts. Its Rights checks are not scored in this version, because the rule verification is still pending (Section~\ref{sec:status}). B5 (learned store router) ran on the current code at 10 OpenAI cases and B0L (a single pass on a larger model) at 10 Gemini cases; both appear in the current-code tables below.


\paragraph{Variants sweep.} Variants S1 to S18 and the ablations are compared with Full on the same backend and the same cases, so each comparison is paired. The offline studies (scheduling S14b and S14c, chunk strategies S16 and S16b, retrieval S4 and S5, removal M17) run at zero model cost. On the OpenAI block (10 dev orders per variant) most single-switch variants leave every metric unchanged: S1, S2, S5, S6, S10, S11 and S12 differ from Full by 0.00 on objection recall and by at most 0.03 elsewhere. S3, S8, S9 and S18 lower objection recall by 0.25 to 0.50 on four orders with intervals that touch zero, S15 lowers answerable coverage by 0.50 ($-1.00$ to $0.00$), and only S4 moves a metric with an interval that excludes zero (citation precision $+0.09$, $0.01$ to $0.19$, ten orders). The Sarvam block was not run; the other scored single-switch variants are the Gemini block B arms above (S11b, S11c, S12b and S15b), where no arm moves objection recall, the unsupported-fact rate or factor recall beyond its 95\% interval at $n=2$. S6 and B5 have no effect until a store prior is trained; the train-order labelling this needs is not done.

\paragraph{Offline studies (no model; retrieval and scheduling).} These run on the real stores at zero model cost, and each says in its output what its targets are. \emph{Retrieval over the citation graph (S5, S4).} On a self-supervised proxy of 150 queries, graph expansion raises recall@10 from 0.037 to 0.537 with the hashed n-gram index and from 0.043 to 0.610 with BGE-M3 vectors (paired differences $+0.50$ and $+0.57$; 95\% intervals $[0.42, 0.58]$ and $[0.49, 0.65]$; all discordant queries favour the expansion, 75 of 75 and 85 of 85). BGE-M3 barely moves plain recall@10 (0.037 to 0.043), so the gain is from the graph, not from the embedder. The proxy's gold is the cited-by relation of the graph that S5 walks, so it favours S5 by construction; the independent G2 set has 8 queries and no positives, so no claim is made on it. The score-skew signal (S4) separates queries whose top ten contain a cited document: AUROC 0.870 (0.751 to 0.969) with the hashed index and 0.672 (0.524 to 0.824) with BGE-M3, on 6 and 7 positive queries, so the two intervals overlap and neither is a stable estimate. \emph{Chunk granularity (S16, S16b; Table~\ref{tab:s16}).} Proposition units give the best recall and MRR at every cutoff on both self-supervised targets, at five times the number of chunks (15,792 against 3,281); late chunking with the hashed embedder is no better than the control, as the true variant needs token vectors from the long-context model. The targets measure findability of a sentence among near-duplicate boilerplate, not recall of court-cited precedents. \emph{Scheduling (S14b, S14c).} In a replay of the 300 T5 cases, first-come-first-served misses the deadline of 66 of 100 planted cases (95\% interval 0.563 to 0.745), while the urgency policy misses none (0.000 to 0.037; McNemar discordant 66/0). Service times are constructed and the policies change only the order, so throughput is identical by construction; this is a simulation, not a serving measurement.

\paragraph{Label-source sensitivity.} The same outputs are scored against the agreed gold, one labeler alone and the keyword rules alone (Table~\ref{tab:table_label_sensitivity}). The ordering of the systems does not change: Full's factor recall stays below B0, B1 and B4 under the keyword rules (0.28 against 0.50, 0.61 and 0.67), under one labeler alone (0.33 against 0.44, 0.56 and 0.67) and under the agreed labels (0.25 against 0.50, 0.63 and 0.63), on 8 to 9 orders each, and every interval overlaps. The conclusion that factor recall is unresolved at this size does not depend on the label source.


\paragraph{Cost.} \resref{table9_cost} gives tokens, store calls, latency and USD per case. Full costs \$0.0107 a case (101,860 tokens, 104 store calls, 280 seconds) against \$0.0138 for B4 (131,239 tokens, a token ratio of 0.78, below the 1.0 to 1.6 the plan expected) and \$0.0004 for B0 (3,862 tokens, about 26 times fewer).
