\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Internal.} Small $n$ and a single run per system limit what can be resolved; an effect smaller than the interval is reported as not resolved. Two exploratory reruns on the development search orders (D12) keep c1457's three losses and change its wins by at most five cells, and an arm compared with its own rerun is decided in 2 of 225 cells; the blind slice was not rerun. The architecture search chose c1457 on 30 development orders, which favours it on those orders; the 20-order holdout and the sealed blind slice, opened once after the freeze tags and the registered hypotheses, are the guard. Thresholds fixed on 50 orders may be mis-set, so margins are rounded to whole points; the pass bar and NLI threshold were fixed before the freeze. The blind scoring code was changed once after a crash, before any score existed (deviation D10).

\paragraph{Construct.} Gold from rules plus an LLM labeler, not humans, risks circularity. The model labeler is an OpenAI model, which was also a system backend in the dev runs (not on the blind slice, which runs Sarvam), so its reading of a factor and the drafter's may share biases; the rule-only gold is the declared sensitivity analysis for this reason, and on the blind slice it changes no hypothesis verdict (Section~\ref{sec:blind}). A second circularity: the system reads the facts part of an order while gold is read from other parts of the same order, and the tagger that builds paragraph metadata is of the same family as the rule labeler. Controls are an 8-word-overlap check, agreed labels, label-source sensitivity, and B6 not reusing the tagger. Because T1 facts are often only a caption, retrieved boilerplate can inflate recall; the answerable-factor rule and the faithful/corroborated table are guards, not a cure. Factor recall against G1 measures what the defence argued, not what a good lawyer should argue; lenient precision against G1 $\cup$ G5 corrects this in part. The unsupported-fact checker (MiniCheck) is independent of the system's checker but was validated on other domains, so subtle legal entailment errors may pass.

\paragraph{External.} One legal domain (bail), one source of orders, one court for the Hindi native set, and constructed data for T2 to T5, T7 and T8 limit transfer. Only 5.9\% of orders split cleanly, so results hold for orders that split cleanly. Qwen3 and the hosted labeler may have seen public orders; the release date bounds the cutoff but does not measure it. There is no user study, and no claim about lawyer time or outcomes.
