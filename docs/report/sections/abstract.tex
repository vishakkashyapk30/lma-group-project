\begin{abstract}
Legal-aid lawyers in India prepare bail applications from messy case files, and a model asked to draft one from memory invents facts and case law. We ask whether an agent harness that plans retrieval per bail factor over typed legal stores, checks every claim against its sources and spars with a simulated prosecutor writes better-grounded bail briefs than simpler systems on the same model. Vakeel-Sahayak.ai has six agents over ten typed stores, two counsel approval gates enforced in code, a pseudonymisation boundary before any hosted model, a Hindi interface, and six recorded demo cases that an evaluator walks from the papers to the export. Gold labels come from court text through a rule labeler and a model labeler that must agree on a verbatim span; nothing is annotated by hand. On development orders an architecture search on Sarvam-105B traced the first full system's losses to four causes and fixed them with four opt-in switches. The resulting system, c1457, was registered with five hypotheses before a sealed slice of 80 test orders was opened once. Against seven baselines (single pass, single store, whole-document and chunked retrieval, query-every-store, a learned store router, an expert template) c1457 has 46 wins, 69 ties and 5 losses (the first full system: 29, 76, 15), with 24 wins and no loss against the single-pass, single-store and whole-document or chunked retrieval baselines B0 to B3. Four of the five hypotheses hold: c1457 beats the full system on objection recall (0.52 to 0.73) and answerable coverage (0.36 to 0.61), cites real authorities where the comparable retrieval baselines fabricate them (fabricated-citation rate 0.00 against 0.64 and 0.95), states a lower share of unsupported facts than all seven baselines, and needs 41\% fewer store calls than querying every store (110 against 188). The fifth, on one unsupported-sentence count, is not supported. The verdicts are the same against rule-only gold labels, and all 320 early export attempts were refused in code. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} lists what the results do not show.
\end{abstract}
