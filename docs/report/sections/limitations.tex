\section{Limitations, Risks and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

This is the one section that lists what the results do not show and what the system does not do. Every other section states what we measured and built; the caveats that qualify those statements are collected here, followed by the work we would do next.

\subsection{What the measurements show}

\paragraph{What M6 measures.} The unsupported-fact rate (M6) checks each argument sentence against the facts of the case alone. c1457's rate of 0.89 is lower than every baseline's, but it means that most argument sentences are not entailed by the facts. On development orders 55\% of the sentences that fail state a legal principle taken from a cited authority, which the facts cannot entail by construction (Section~\ref{sec:arch}); 24\% say what the record does not state and 7\% carry a pseudonym label, so about 9\% are real mismatches. On case-fact sentences alone (M6a) the rate on the development search orders is 0.47 (Full: 0.84). M6 is therefore a relative measure between systems, not an absolute measure of fabrication.

\paragraph{Citation precision.} c1457's citation precision on the blind slice is 0.71: about three in ten cited paragraphs do not support the sentence that cites them, although none is invented. On the same slice c1457 loses precision to Full (0.85), and it loses citation recall and the unfinished-factor share to Full, the cost of drafting from the facts text and of listing unmet factors instead of filling them.

\paragraph{One model, one slice, one run per system.} The blind result rests on 80 sealed orders, one run per system, and one hosted model, Sarvam-105B. Sarvam calls run at temperature 0 but are not deterministic. Two reruns on the 30 development search orders (deviation D12, exploratory, no registered test) keep c1457's three losses and move its wins by at most five cells, but outputs change per order: c1457's unsupported-fact rate differs between the two reruns on 62\% of orders and its citation precision on 93\%. The blind slice itself was not rerun. The development runs on OpenAI and Gemini used earlier code and 2 to 50 orders per arm, so they show direction, not a ranking across model families. c1457 was chosen on 30 development orders and confirmed on a 20-order holdout before the blind slice was opened once, so its development numbers are selected. Thresholds fixed on 50 orders may be mis-set; margins are rounded to whole points. The blind scoring code was changed once after a crash and before any score existed (deviation D10).

\paragraph{Gold labels are not human.} Gold comes from court text, official tables and the agreement of a rule labeler and a model labeler, never from annotators. Agreement is moderate: $\kappa$ is 0.60 for G1, weakest on gravity (0.27) and weak prima facie case (0.25), and 0.84 for G3, weakest on previous bail (0.26); the agreed G1 gold leaves 81 of 250 orders without a factor, and G7 has a label for 1 of 250 orders. The model labeler is an OpenAI model that was also a system backend in the development runs, so its reading of a factor and the drafter's may share biases; the blind slice ran Sarvam, and the rule-only gold, which changes no hypothesis verdict, is the declared check. Gold is read from other parts of the same order whose facts part the system reads, and the tagger that builds paragraph metadata belongs to the same family as the rule labeler. Factor recall against G1 measures what the defence argued, not what a good lawyer should argue. MiniCheck is independent of the system's checker but was validated on other domains, so a subtle legal entailment error may pass. Only 37.3\% of extracted citations resolve to a document, no development order has a resolved court citation (recall of court-cited precedents is measurable on the test slice only, 14 of 200 orders), and only 8 of 50 development orders have a gold factor that the facts make answerable.

\paragraph{Thin facts and transfer.} The facts part of a T1 order is often only a caption (19 of 50 development orders have under 1,000 characters). A single pass then answers ``no facts supplied'', while Full draws grounds from retrieved authorities not tied to the client's facts, so Full can win on recall through retrieved boilerplate; the answerable-factor rule and the faithful/corroborated table limit this but do not remove it. Only 5.9\% of orders split cleanly into facts and the rest, so the results hold for orders that split cleanly. The study covers one legal domain (bail), one source of orders, one court and two years for the native Hindi set (HLDC was unavailable), and constructed data for T2 to T5, T7 and T8. Qwen3 and the hosted labeler may have seen public orders; the release date bounds the cutoff but does not measure it. There is no user study, and we make no claim about lawyer time or outcomes.

\subsection{The sparring token budget}

\paragraph{Frozen runs.} In the frozen blind runs 68\% of c1457's 599 objections were never judged: 406 objections, of which 394 were refused for budget, 11 by the privacy boundary and 1 for a model error, because drafting used up the factor's or the case's token budget before the reply and judging calls ran. The system records such an objection as unanswered for counsel. The 599 objections end as 58 answered (10\%), 26 partly answered (4\%), 109 judged unanswered (18\%) and the 406 never judged, so the 86\% unanswered figure combines judged and unjudged objections, describes a sparring step starved of tokens, and is not a measure of reply quality. Of the 109 judged unanswered, 79 say the reply restates or gives no facts or authority, 26 carry the text of a reply that could not be supported, and 36 name the NDPS s.37 bar, which can also be raised in orders that do not mention NDPS because the judge has no label for ``does not apply''. The objection recall and answerable coverage that c1457 achieves (0.73 and 0.61) are measured on this starved step. We found no fault in the judge or in how objections are parsed.

\paragraph{The reserve added after the freeze.} After the freeze we added an opt-in per-case reserve of tokens for sparring (\texttt{sparring\_reserve\_tokens}, on in the product at 200,000), which judges each reply right after it is drafted and marks an objection it cannot cover as left for counsel. It is not part of the frozen system and was not rerun on the sealed slice. Measured on the development search orders, 27\% of objections were judged with no reserve (the D12 first rerun: 216 objections, 158 not judged). A reserve of 60,000 tokens judged 17\% (7 of 41 objections on 7 orders; the run was stopped there because the reserve was spent before judging, about 60,000 per order against 18,000 to 25,000 for raising objections and 36,000 to 42,000 for replies and repairs). A reserve of 120,000 with judging after all replies judged 60 of 61 objections (98\%) on the first 8 development orders of a run that we stopped to apply the change below; these orders raise about 7 objections each, whereas the synthetic demo cases raise 13 to 16, and on those six demo recordings the same setting judged 8 of 67 objections. With the 200,000 reserve and judging after each reply, the six final demo recordings judged 53 of 68 objections and left 15 for counsel; case 01 left 11 of 16 (the reserve check estimates up to 26,000 tokens for a reply, its repair and its judgement, and 22,000 were left). The demo recordings are six synthetic cases on one model and are not an evaluation.

The final measurement of the reserve on the 30 development orders (Section~\ref{sec:system}, Table~\ref{tab:reserve}; \texttt{docs/results/search/\allowbreak final\_report/\allowbreak reserve\_dev.md}) raised the judged share from 26\% to 97\%, and six limits qualify it. First, the arm without a reserve runs the current code, not the frozen code, so the comparison with the frozen D12 run (27\% judged) is indirect. Second, each arm ran once and the model is not deterministic (the D12 reruns of c1457 against B0 to B6 gave 30/31/3 and 35/28/3). Third, the scorer does not score the judge's verdicts, so the gain shows in what counsel sees at Gate~2 and in no quality metric. Fourth, over-search is the one significant paired difference and it is higher with the reserve (+0.0152, interval $[+0.0046, +0.0276]$, surviving Holm correction); the reserve arm also has one more loss against the baselines B0 to B6 (4 against 3), over-search against B5, besides confident wrong against B4 and unfinished factors against B4 and B5, which the arm without a reserve also loses. Fifth, the 6 objections not judged with the reserve were 5 privacy-boundary refusals and 1 model error. Sixth, we did not rerun the sealed slice with the reserve, and the 30 development orders raise about 7 objections each against 13 to 16 in the demo cases, so the result does not carry to them: case 01 of the demos left 11 of 16 objections for counsel.

\subsection{Statute data and the Rights Radar}

\paragraph{Empty statutory position in the frozen runs.} The brief's structured statutory position (sections, bailability, maximum punishment) is empty in all 80 blind briefs of every tool-using system: the hosted extractor answered with the phrase of the order where the schema asks for one object per section, and every such answer was rejected as malformed. Counsel is then asked under which sections the accused is charged, and an objection can misstate the punishment (on t1dev-019, the simulated prosecutor called three-year offences ``punishable up to life imprisonment'', and a reply borrowed a custody period from the cited authority and was marked for review). We fixed the extraction after the freeze, in the product and not in the frozen code, and checked it on one order; the blind slice was not rerun.

\paragraph{Statute-store bail flags.} After the runs we found that the bail flags the statute store took from the CrPC First Schedule on 26 October are the wrong way round for 43 of the 398 IPC sections (IPC 364, kidnapping in order to murder, is marked bailable), from an error in reading the printed table. In the frozen runs the dossier's sections were empty, so the flags could reach a brief only through the simulated prosecutor's statute lookups. The schedule was re-extracted by script and the store rebuilt from it on 13 November; the frozen runs keep the store they were made with.

\paragraph{Maximum-punishment lookup.} On 15 November we found two more faults in how the Rights Radar reads the maximum punishment. For BNS 109 with 3(5), the lookup dropped the maximum of the whole charge because the common-intention section has no punishment of its own; for NDPS clauses it found no row for ``21(a)'' and the stored row holds the section's heaviest term (20 years), which is wrong for a small-quantity charge. Both are fixed in the product: a section that only carries the punishment of the offence is left out, and a quantity-band clause is read from the section text (21(a) 1 year, 21(b) 10 years, 21(c) 20 years). Conspiracy (BNS 61(2), IPC 120B) still leaves the maximum unknown, Part II of the First Schedule is not applied to NDPS, and a bare NDPS section leaves the radar at CHECK until counsel reads the clause and the quantity from the charge sheet. The frozen runs predate these fixes.

\paragraph{Rights Radar scope.} Seven of the seven rules match the statute store in an automatic check of each cited section's number, heading, sub-section and the conditions the rule tests (\texttt{scripts/check\_rights\_rules.py}); the check reads acts only, not the judgments a rule also relies on, and the store's Companies Act s.212 comes from a copy of the Ministry's text dated March 2016, so a later amendment would not be seen. A rule's verification is bound to a hash of its text and code, so a later change clears it, and an unverified rule is printed in the export as ``Not yet checked against the bare act''. T4 is generated from the same rule table, so it measures conformance of the code to the rule text, not legal accuracy; the scenarios and the code share one reading of the rules. No hosted or local model was run on T4, so the Radar was not compared with one, and the two comparison rows are an offline reader, not a language model. In demo case 01 the radar applied the half threshold of 1,278 days where the one-third threshold of 852 days applies to a first-time offender, so s.479 reads NOT\_YET for a client who may already be eligible; counsel reads the antecedents.

\subsection{Runs that were not made and historical outputs}

\paragraph{RQ2 and fine-tuning.} The ingestion arms on degraded, photographed and Hindi pages (S7, P5 to P8 on T2) ran only with offline stand-ins and were not scored. The QLoRA pipeline for a 4B dossier extractor is built and unit-tested, but no model was trained for lack of GPU time, so A5 has nothing tuned to compare with. The tool-calling set T6 and the injection set T7 were not run on the hosted model, so tool selection and attack success are not measured, and S13 and the retrieval and scheduling variants were measured without a model. The 4B and 14B models of the design were not run on the GPU nodes; serving Qwen3-32B on the shared nodes failed and was dropped.

\paragraph{Stand-in arms.} The offline stand-in arms (the store knock-outs A10, the intake arms S7 and P5 to P8, the injection arm S17) carry that label in the tables and measure plumbing, not a language model. Removing any store empties the stand-in's brief, so the seven A10 knock-outs score identically.

\paragraph{Offline studies.} The citation-graph proxy (S5) takes its gold from the cited-by relation that S5 walks, so it favours S5 by construction, and the independent G2 set for it has 8 queries and no positives, so we make no claim on it. The score-skew signal (S4) rests on 6 and 7 positive queries, and its two intervals overlap, so neither is a stable estimate. The chunk-granularity targets measure the findability of a sentence among near-duplicate boilerplate, not recall of court-cited precedents. The scheduling replay (S14) uses constructed service times and is a simulation, not a serving measurement. On the development blocks, S6 and B5 had no effect until a store prior was trained, and the train-order labelling this needs was not done. The Gemini block B stopped when the free-tier daily quota was spent, so it is a directional first look.

\paragraph{M6a historical outputs (D13, D14).} The offline A10 arms stop after retrieval and draft nothing, so their count of case-fact sentences is 0 by definition and their M6a is undefined. In D13 we set the 70 blank cells to 0 in the committed results file, a repair of a file and not of a system; the raw offline outputs no longer exist. The six Gemini 3.1 arms (A4, A5, S11b, S11c, S12b, S15b) had no recoverable outputs, so in D14 we reran them on 15 November on two development orders with the settings of the block. The reruns are new samples of a non-deterministic model: their M6 and other metrics differ from the values first scored, their gaps to Full (which was not rerun) include run-to-run variation, an interval built from two orders is not resolved, and A5 is effectively a second sample of Full because every role maps to the same model.

\subsection{Privacy, access and cost}

\paragraph{Privacy boundary.} Client files stay on the user's machine and external model services are off by default. On 250 T1 orders no FIR number, case number or numeric date survived the boundary, and it refused 5 orders whose names OCR glued to other words, so one order of 250 stays local. We do not claim zero leakage: the scrubber is rule-based and Hindi coverage is thin. On the blind slice the boundary also refused 175 outgoing payloads of c1457 that still carried a personal name. The privacy boundary refused some development orders for hosted models (8 of the first 25, 26 of 100 baseline case-runs); the local fallback was not running, so those cases were scored as empty briefs and every paired comparison uses only the cases both arms answered.

\paragraph{Paid providers, free tiers and outcome predictions.} OpenAI and Sarvam are locked in code unless the owner names them; both were opened one at a time under a cap. Free tiers receive public court text only, never client text. The system makes no outcome predictions, shows no grant rates and gives no advice to accused persons or the public.

\paragraph{Hindi interface.} The Hindi interface strings were translated by a free model (Gemma on the Gemini free tier) and not reviewed by a lawyer; text inside the tabs, counts and names inside a sentence, and nine recorded tour texts remain English, and the case's own text is not translated.

\paragraph{No authentication.} The gates are enforced in code, but there are no user accounts and the API token is off by default, so ``approval only from counsel'' is a property of the code path, not of an identity check.

\paragraph{Budgets and compute.} About 4.15 USD was spent on OpenAI (locked in code again) and about INR 990 on Sarvam by 13 November, under an INR 5,000 cap that the ledger enforces. The free Gemini tier allows 500 requests a day per project on the flash-lite models, about 10 Full cases a day (a Full case takes about 49 requests); the full plan of about 4,500 Full case-runs does not fit, so sample sizes were cut, not features. Gemma 4 31B failed on 86 of 94 calls in an hour, scoring one block of about 500 case-runs with MiniCheck took over three hours on a CPU, and the laptop is at 97\% disk use.

\paragraph{Known weaknesses.} Grounds are sometimes weak in substance (about ten open issues per case). Some briefs carry a sentence that borrows a fact from a cited authority; the checker marks it for review but does not remove it. The brief is a first draft for counsel, which is how the gates treat it.

\subsection{Future work}

We would do the following, in order of expected value.
First, rerun the sealed slice with the sparring reserve and the statute fixes, so that the objection-reply metrics describe a sparring step that is not starved and the statutory position is measured on 80 orders.
Second, size the reserve per case, and raise fewer objections per case, so that no objection is left for counsel only for lack of budget; and reduce the over-search that the reserve adds in the sparring step.
Third, read NDPS quantity bands from the charge sheet, so that the radar computes default bail and the s.479 limit for a bare NDPS section, and let the checker reject objections and replies that misstate punishment.
Fourth, ground the prosecutor: check Abhiyojak's objections against the statute store and the facts, and drop those that do not apply, such as an NDPS bar in an IPC case.
Fifth, repeat the blind comparison with several runs per system, to put an interval on run-to-run variance, and on a second model family.
Sixth, separate legal principles from case facts in the metric and the draft, checking law sentences against the cited paragraph and fact sentences against the record.
Seventh, train the 4B QLoRA dossier extractor (the pipeline and 110 tests exist), run A5 against it, score the ingestion arms on T2 with the real models, and run T6 and T7 on the hosted model.
Eighth, let legal-aid lawyers use the interface on real files under consent, with authentication in front of the gates.
