\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

\paragraph{Agents.} \textbf{Munshi} (intake) decides the parse route of each page, when to escalate, document boundaries and types, where each item is filed, and what is missing. \textbf{Peshkar} builds the dossier with a source quote for every field, a List of Dates, and the Rights Radar (deterministic checks on custody periods and statutory rights, each with a verification status). \textbf{Sutradhar} orchestrates retrieval. \textbf{Vakeel} drafts the grounds and checks and repairs every claim. \textbf{Abhiyojak} plays the prosecution with its own prompt and an allow-listed context. \textbf{Pairokar} orders a batch by urgency and decides per case between a full brief, a flag for counsel, or a request for documents. Every action is appended to an event log; dossier, timeline, brief and sparring record are views of it, which gives an audit trail and the dependency data for truth maintenance.

\paragraph{Ingestion ladder.} Munshi handles ten input kinds (born-digital, scanned, degraded, phone photo, Hindi or mixed script, legacy Hindi fonts, handwriting, bundles, locked files, non-PDF). A page climbs only as far as needed: text layer; layout parser; clean-up and OCR; a vision model asked only to transcribe; a request to counsel for a clearer copy. Per-page quality signals (text coverage, OCR confidence, dictionary-word share, script, legacy-font patterns) decide each step and are logged. A gap-driven re-read returns to a page when a checklist field is still missing. The ladder is evaluated as a router against the cheapest route that clears the quality bar.

\paragraph{Ten stores on four shelves.} A brief needs exact text, a section-mapping graph, court reasoning, citation history, what each side usually argues, and the client's own record; each has a different right way to search. The \emph{law} shelf holds statutes (exact lookup, BM25) and the IPC--BNS and CrPC--BNSS concordance (typed graph). The \emph{case-law} shelf holds reasoning paragraphs (BM25 plus a dense vector, metadata pre-filter), the citation graph with treatment and court level, and whole-order fact vectors. The \emph{advocacy} shelf holds defence-argument and prosecution-objection paragraphs by factor, used for coverage and phrasing, never cited as authority. The private \emph{case} shelf holds the client's documents in structure-aware chunks, a dated timeline and the dossier. The October 2026 build has 161,708 reasoning paragraphs, 70,880 arguments, 55,161 objections, 114,692 similar-fact orders and 5,842 citation edges. Test-window orders and same-FIR orders are excluded from every store. The dense side of the runs reported here is a hashed character n-gram vector. BGE-M3 side vectors for the four vector stores were built on a GPU node (2\,h\,43\,min, 1.65\,GB) and are opt-in (\texttt{VS\_STORES=bge}); retrieval under BGE-M3 is measured in the offline studies of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Evidence contracts and the orchestrator.} Each of twelve bail factors has a contract: slots (for example co-accused order, role comparison, authority, good-law check, likely objection), a need for each (required, a count, or optional), the stores that may hold it, and a budget in store calls and tokens. Sutradhar decides whether the factor applies, the order of calls, the query and filters, whether a result is good enough, when to relax a filter or follow a citation, and when to stop; slots left unfilled become open issues, never silent omissions. A learned store prior, trained from train orders, may order calls within a contract (S6) or replace the contracts (B5).

\paragraph{Checking and truth maintenance.} Vakeel checks every claim with an NLI model and rules (statute lookup, section mapping, good-law check), and repairs or flags it; nothing is dropped silently. Each claim records the dossier fields (by hash), timeline events, rights entries and authorities it depends on. When counsel approves a change, claims with a changed dependency, and claims citing them, become stale; only the reopened factors are re-planned, and unchanged claims stay byte-identical (tested).

\paragraph{Gates and privacy.} Gate 1 (dossier) and Gate 2 (brief) pass only on counsel's approval, and export is impossible before Gate 2, in code and in tests. Raw client files stay on the user's machine. External model services are off by default, gated by a mode (\texttt{off}, \texttt{public}, \texttt{pseudonymised}, \texttt{consented}); client text needs counsel's per-case consent event for the provider even when pseudonymised, and every external payload passes the boundary. The boundary aliases names and quasi-identifiers (FIR, case and crime numbers, police stations, villages, addresses, PIN codes, vehicle numbers, ages) and shifts every date by a per-case offset; on the 250 T1 orders no FIR number, case number or numeric date survived, and the boundary keeps the calls for 5 orders whose names OCR glued to other words on the local machine. Gate 2's approval is bound to a hash of the brief, sparring record and List of Dates, so a later change re-closes export. Defences against instructions planted in documents (a typed hand-off from a tool-less extractor, spotlighting, a guard) are all on by default; switching each off is an arm of the safety test set T7.

\paragraph{Models and where they run.} The design specifies a 4B model with OCR and a vision model on an 8\,GB laptop GPU for Munshi and Peshkar, and a 14B model on shared GPU nodes for Sutradhar, Vakeel and Abhiyojak. The language-model runs reported here are hosted (Sarvam-105B for the blind slice and the architecture search, OpenAI and Gemini for development blocks) or offline stand-ins, and every hosted call goes through the pseudonymisation boundary; the Gemini free tier receives public court text only. Paid providers are locked in code and were opened one at a time with the owner's go-ahead and a spend cap, OpenAI for the OpenAI runs and Sarvam for the Sarvam runs; OpenAI is locked again.

\paragraph{Sparring reserve.} Abhiyojak raises objections and Vakeel drafts a reply to each, so drafting and sparring draw on one token budget. An opt-in field of the switch object, \texttt{sparring\_reserve\_tokens}, sets aside a per-case reserve for sparring and judges each reply right after Vakeel drafts it. An objection that the remaining reserve cannot cover is not dropped: it stays on the Gate~2 checklist and in the exported objections sheet with the reason that no reply was drafted because the sparring budget was spent, so counsel answers it. The product runs with a reserve of 200{,}000 tokens; the default behaviour of every evaluation variant is unchanged. In the six final demo recordings, 53 of 68 objections received a reply that Abhiyojak judged (Section~\ref{sec:limitations} gives the measured status).

\paragraph{Evaluator demo.} Six recorded bail cases load when the API starts (the switch \texttt{VS\_DEMO\_CASES} turns this off). Each is synthetic, with role pseudonyms only, and each was run end to end through the real API on Sarvam-105B exactly as the web interface calls it. The Cases page lists them, and a guided tour walks one case in nine steps, from intake and the dossier through Gate~1, the Rights Radar, the brief, sparring and hearing preparation to Gate~2 and the exported file. The tour is read-only and advances with Next; every field is already filled, each caption states what the agent did in that recorded run with the run's own numbers, and the tour ends with a real Gate~2 approval, with the acknowledgements the checklist requires, followed by the DOCX download. A reset route puts a case back as recorded for the next evaluator. The six cases differ by offence and law (IPC, BNS, NDPS small and commercial quantity), by custody and rights (default bail open, closed or not yet open; the s.37 bar), by record quality (a complete file, a Hindi FIR, an image-only page read by OCR, four papers only) and by parity and history (a co-accused granted bail, refused, or none). The recordings cost INR 36.67 in all (85 to 139 hosted calls each).

\paragraph{Hindi interface.} A toggle in the top bar switches the interface between English and Hindi. The shell, the stepper, the status badges, every step's title, intro and footer button, the gate lists and the tour bar are translated, as are the captions of all six tours (1{,}010 strings, 131 of them unchanged); the translations were made with a free model on public interface text.
