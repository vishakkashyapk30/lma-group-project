\section{Main Result: the Blind Slice}
\label{sec:blind}

\paragraph{Protocol.} The system tested here, c1457, is the first full system (Full) with four opt-in fixes found by the architecture search on development orders (Section~\ref{sec:arch}): X1 fills dossier gaps with rules over the raw facts, X4 moves sentences that still fail after repair into open issues for counsel, X5 drafts from the raw facts while the dossier still drives planning and Gate~1, and X7 resolves an objection's reference to offered material by its id. The freeze tags \texttt{prereg-v1} and \texttt{eval-freeze-v1} were created and pushed on 9 November before the slice was opened; the claims for c1457 (Table~\ref{tab:hyp}) are registered in Section~1.5 of the pre-registration, with the deviations of its Section~8 (80 blind orders, one run per system, Sarvam-105B only). c1457, Full and B0--B6 ran once from the freeze tag. The single gated scoring call crashed in the claim checker on a draft sentence longer than its 512-token window, before writing any score. The scorer was fixed (a sentence that does not fit is checked in pieces and the least supported piece decides; every other sentence scores exactly as before, which a byte-identical rescoring of a scored dev arm confirms), the saved outputs were hashed, and the slice was scored once more (deviation D10). A comparison is a win only when the paired-bootstrap interval at its Holm level excludes zero (10{,}000 resamples; Holm at 0.05 over B0--B6 for each arm and metric).

\paragraph{Result.} On the 80 blind orders c1457 has 46 wins, 69 ties and 5 losses against B0--B6; Full has 29, 76 and 15 (Table~\ref{tab:arch_wlt}, rows ``blind''). Four of the five registered hypotheses hold (Table~\ref{tab:hyp}). c1457 beats Full on objection recall (0.52 to 0.73) and answerable coverage (0.36 to 0.61). It beats B4 and B5, the two retrieval baselines of comparable capability, on citation precision (0.71 against 0.02 and 0.10) and on the fabricated-citation rate (0.00 against 0.95 and 0.64). Its unsupported-fact rate (0.89) is lower than every baseline's (0.94 to 1.00), a win against all seven. It needs fewer store calls than B4 (110 against 188 per order). H-A1 is falsified: besides the four cells declared structural before the opening (all four are losses), c1457 loses the count of unsupported sentences against B5 (6.0 against 4.2 per order), because B5 writes fewer sentences (4.4 against 7.0); on the rate c1457 beats B5. Head to head, c1457 has 8 wins, 13 ties and 3 losses against Full: it loses citation precision (0.85 to 0.71), citation recall and the unfinished-factor share, the price of drafting from the facts text and of listing unmet factors instead of filling them. The verdicts are the same with errored records kept as scored and with the two cases rerun under deviation D7 dropped.

\paragraph{What the numbers mean in absolute terms.} The win on the unsupported-fact rate is relative: 0.89 means that most argument sentences are still not entailed by the facts of the case alone. On development orders 55\% of such sentences state a legal principle taken from a cited authority, which the facts cannot entail by construction (Section~\ref{sec:arch}), so the rate overstates fabrication; on case-fact sentences alone the rate on the development search orders is 0.47 (Full: 0.84). Citation precision of 0.71 means about three in ten cited paragraphs do not support the sentence that cites them, although none is invented.

\paragraph{Does the result depend on the gold labels?} The agreed gold keeps a label only when the rule labeler and the model labeler agree on a verbatim span. The pre-registration declares one sensitivity check against this choice: the same saved outputs scored against the rule labeler alone, with the same scoring code (deviation D11, run once after the primary scoring). On the rule-only gold c1457 has 45 wins, 70 ties and 5 losses against B0--B6 (agreed gold: 46, 69, 5) and Full has 29, 74 and 17 (29, 76, 15). Five of the 350 arm-by-baseline-by-metric cells change outcome (one c1457 win against B3 on court-discussed recall becomes a tie); no loss appears or disappears for c1457, and all five hypothesis verdicts are the same on both golds. The result does not hinge on the model labeler.

\paragraph{Does the result survive a rerun?} Every system above ran once, and the Sarvam calls run at temperature 0 but are not deterministic. The pre-registration adds one exploratory check (deviation D12, no registered test): c1457, Full and B0--B6 ran twice more on the 30 development search orders, from the frozen code with the response cache off, scored with the same code on the agreed gold (the blind and holdout slices were not touched). Against B0--B6 c1457 has 30 wins, 31 ties and 3 losses in the first rerun and 35, 28 and 3 in the second (the original search run: 30, 37, 3), and the three losses are the same three cells each time (the unfinished-factor share against B4 and B5, the confident-wrong rate against B4). Full has 10, 47, 7 and 11, 47, 8 (original: 11, 49, 10); its losses move between runs, including two new losses on the unsupported-fact rate. Compared with its own rerun under the same decision rule, no arm differs except B5 on two of its 25 metrics, so 2 of 225 arm-by-metric cells are decided where none should be. Per order the outputs do change: c1457's unsupported-fact rate differs between the two reruns on 62\% of orders (mean absolute difference 0.13; means 0.89 and 0.87) and citation precision on 93\% (0.74 and 0.71), while factor recall, objection recall and the pass verdict (factor recall at least 0.5) change on no order. The aggregate comparisons are stable across runs; single-order outputs are not, which is one more reason counsel reviews every brief (\texttt{docs/results/search/final\_report/summary.md}, \texttt{variance\_d12.csv}).

\paragraph{Tool calling and unapproved actions.} Table~\ref{tab:tools} counts, from the saved event logs, how the four tool-using systems call their tools on the blind slice. c1457 makes about 149 tool calls per order over 17 tools; 1.0\% are refused for a bad argument (for example a claim check that names a dossier field that does not exist), fewer than Full (3.1\%) and the two retrieval baselines (4.9\% and 6.4\%). A refused call returns a typed error and the agent continues; every order with a tool or model error still ends with a complete, scored brief. Each system tries to export once per order before counsel has approved the brief; all 320 attempts are refused in code and no export is executed, so the unapproved-action rate is 0 of 320. The privacy boundary refused 175 outgoing payloads of c1457 that still carried an un-pseudonymised personal name (most often a name inside a quoted sentence or retrieved authority); none of them was sent. We did not measure tool \emph{selection} against a gold tool sequence: the T6 tool-calling set was not run on the hosted model.

\paragraph{A worked case.} Development order t1dev-019 (Andhra Pradesh High Court, anticipatory bail, Sections 143, 341, 332 and 353 read with 34 IPC; 871 characters of facts). Peshkar's dossier finds almost nothing in so short a record: ten fields stay unsupported at Gate~1 and become questions for counsel (date of arrest, first remand, charge sheet, antecedents). Sutradhar plans five factors; for each it queries the stores its contract names until a budget or the sufficiency rule stops it (115 store calls). Vakeel drafts four grounds. The weak-prima-facie ground cites \emph{Kunal v.\ State} (Delhi High Court, 12 March 2024, para 7: at the bail stage the court only sees a prima facie case and holds no mini trial); the checker finds the cited paragraph supports the sentence (entailment 0.99). The gravity and witness-tampering grounds each fail their first check on a sentence and are repaired against the retrieved authority before counsel sees them. The antecedents ground can only say that the record is silent, so X4 removes it from the argument and lists it as an open issue (``no supported argument could be drafted for antecedents: counsel to draft it''). Abhiyojak then raises six objections. The weak-prima-facie objection is answered from the same \emph{Kunal} paragraph, and the antecedents objection with an order stating the absence of criminal history; both are judged answered. Gravity and witness tampering are judged partly answered (the reply cites law but no facts of this case), and two stay unanswered. Gate~1 was approved by scripted counsel; Gate~2 was not, and export was refused.

\paragraph{Failure examples.} The same order shows four ways the system fails; the counts show how often two of them occur on the blind slice.
(1) \emph{A borrowed fact.} The reply to the flight-risk objection says the petitioners ``have remained in custody for about 17 months'' and tags the facts of the case as its source. The facts say nothing about custody (this is an anticipatory bail petition); the number comes from the cited authority. The checker could not verify the sentence and marked the reply for review, but did not remove it; counsel must catch it.
(2) \emph{An objection that does not apply.} Abhiyojak raises the NDPS s.37 statutory bar in an IPC case, and the reply restates the procedural posture instead of saying the bar does not apply. On the blind slice 86\% of c1457's 599 objections stay unanswered and 10\% are answered.
(3) \emph{An overstated objection.} The gravity objection calls the offences ``punishable up to life imprisonment''; none of the four sections carries more than three years. The objection is the simulated prosecutor's, so it is not checked against the statute store.
(4) \emph{An empty statutory position.} The brief's structured statutory position (sections, bailability, maximum punishment) is empty although the order names the sections, and it is empty in all 80 blind briefs of every tool-using system: the section field of the dossier is never filled on the hosted runs, so counsel is asked ``under which sections is the accused charged?''. The event logs show why: the hosted extractor did find the sections, but answered with the phrase of the order (``Sections 143, 341, 332 and 353 read with 34 IPC'') where the schema asks for one object per section, and every such answer was rejected as malformed. After the freeze we fixed this in the product (not in the frozen code that produced the results above): a phrase is read with the rule parser of the offline reader, one section per value, each still checked against its quote. Rerun on this order with c1457, the dossier holds IPC 143, 341, 332, 353 and 34, the statutory position reads ``not bailable, maximum punishment 3 years'' from the statute store, counsel is no longer asked for the sections, and Abhiyojak's gravity objection now says ``punishable with imprisonment up to three years'' instead of life imprisonment, which also removes failure (3) on this order. We did not rerun the blind slice with the fix.
