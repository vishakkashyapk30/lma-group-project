\section{Architecture search}
\label{sec:arch}

\paragraph{Why Full loses.} On the development runs Full does not beat every baseline (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Reading its outputs gives four causes. (1) The unsupported-fact rate checks every argument sentence against the facts of the case only; in Full's flagged sentences 55\% state a legal principle from a cited authority, 24\% say what the record does not state and 7\% carry a pseudonym label, so only about 9\% are real mismatches with the facts. (2) Munshi and Peshkar fill about 2.8 of the 13 dossier fields per case, and the rule-based stance picker never produces a custody-period, parity or trial-delay ground. (3) Abhiyojak's objections are checked against the material it was offered by an exact store label; on Sarvam Full kept 66 objections and discarded 230, and 263 of 320 dropped references named a paragraph that had in fact been offered. (4) Sentences that fail the check stay in the argument, marked, instead of becoming open issues.

\paragraph{Candidates.} Each fix is an opt-in switch with the default byte-identical (Section~\ref{sec:system}): X1 fills dossier gaps with rules over the raw facts; X2 splits case-fact sentences (near-verbatim spans of the record) from legal-basis sentences; X3 adds one Abhiyojak call on factors with no ground; X4 removes sentences that still fail after repair, and ``the record is silent'' sentences, from the argument and lists them as open issues for counsel; X5 lets Vakeel draft from the raw facts while the dossier still drives the planner and Gate~1; X6 searches Abhiyojak's stores a second time keyed on the facts; X7 resolves an objection's reference to offered material by its id; X8 replaces a case-fact sentence that paraphrases the record by the record sentence.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Sarvam-105B behind the pseudonymisation boundary. Search on the 30 dev orders t1dev-000..029 (single switches, then combinations); the 20 dev orders t1dev-030..049 are a holdout touched once, for the winner, Full and the baselines; the blind slice of the test set is run once from the freeze tag. Every arm of the final search table was rerun on one commit. A comparison is a win only when the paired-bootstrap interval at its Holm level excludes zero (10{,}000 resamples, seed 0; Holm at 0.05 over B0--B6 for each arm and metric). Quality metrics decide; cost is judged only against B4 and B5, the baselines of comparable capability; a metric a baseline cannot produce (objection precision when it raises no objection, the case-fact rate M6a when it writes no sentence markers) is listed as undefined, not won.

\gentable{arch_wlt}

\paragraph{Result.} The winner, c1457, is Full with X1, X4, X5 and X7 (Table~\ref{tab:arch_wlt}). On the 30 search orders it has 30 wins, 37 ties and 3 losses against B0--B6, where Full has 11, 49 and 10. Its objection recall rises from 0.36 to 1.00 (X7: the objections were there and were being discarded), answerable coverage from 0.38 to 0.61, coverage from 0.64 to 0.97, and the unsupported-fact rate falls from 0.99 to 0.90 (case-fact sentences alone: 0.84 to 0.47). It pays with citation precision (0.82 to 0.68, still a win over B4 and B5, which fabricate citations on 93\% and 64\% of orders) and 27\% more tokens. X2 failed on Sarvam (most grounds could not meet the verbatim rule and came out empty) and X8 added a loss for three more wins, so neither is in the winner. On the holdout the winner repeats: 22 wins, 26 ties, 3 losses, the same three cells (Full: 10, 40 and 5).

\paragraph{The losses that remain are structural.} (1) Unfinished-factor share against B4 and B5: the winner plans 130 factors on the search orders and 47 end with a required slot missing (trial stage, witness examination, antecedents, community roots). A search of the facts text (not the gold) finds none of these 62 slots stated in the record, so ``missing'' is the true state; B4 and B5 have no required slots and score near zero by construction. Filling the slots would be fabrication; not planning those factors would drop the prosecution concerns counsel must prepare for. (2) Confident-wrong rate against B4: the metric counts a sentence as faithful only when the facts of the case support it, so a sentence stating a legal principle counts as wrong unless the court's order happens to repeat it; B4 emits 7.5\% of its grounds, a selected few sentences. (3) Coverage against B6, a template that scores 1.00 by construction (a loss over the pooled 50 orders and on the blind slice). Over the pooled 50 orders the winner also loses confident-wrong against B5 (B5's few sentences are drawn from the order's own grounds). None of these is chased, because closing them would change what the metric counts, not the brief.

\gentable{arch_cost}

\paragraph{Cost.} Table~\ref{tab:arch_cost}: the winner needs fewer store calls than B4 (109 against 186 per order on the search orders, 110 against 188 on the blind slice, a win each time) and as many tokens (a tie); it is slower than both and uses more tokens and store calls than B5. Against the one-call baselines and the template its cost is necessarily higher and is shown, not counted.

\gentable{arch_ablate}

\paragraph{Ablations of the winner.} Table~\ref{tab:arch_ablate} compares the winner with the winner minus one of its switches and with A1--A4 and A6--A9 applied on top of it (A1, A2, A8 and A9 drop the winner switch they cannot combine with, which the switch validator refuses). Removing X4 costs four wins and gains none; removing X1 or X5 changes nothing that the 30 orders can resolve (X5 alone lowered the unsupported-fact rate in the single-switch round, which X4 and X1 now largely cover). Without check and repair (A2, A9) the winner gains five and six wins; without sparring (A8) two; no tools (A3), plain retrieval (A4) and no dossier (A1) change nothing the 30 orders resolve. Two rows go against the winner and neither is a better brief. Removing X1 wins the unsupported-sentence count because it writes fewer sentences (5.1 against 6.8 per order, with the same number supported). The free planner (A7) beats the winner on the unsupported-fact rate (0.50 against 0.90), the unsupported count, the confident-wrong rate and the unfinished-factor share, and against B0--B6 its only loss is coverage against the template. Three independent read-only checks of the saved outputs found the gains are measurement, not quality. The free planner records no plan or assessment, so the unfinished-factor share has nothing to count (88 contract gaps passed to counsel become 1). Its model rarely searches the precedent stores, so Vakeel restates the facts text under the factor headings: 18\% of its grounds cite an authority against 90\%, 19 of 30 briefs cite none, and it writes 29 fewer grounds and twice as many ``counsel to draft it'' notes. The like-for-like case-fact rate (M6a) is a tie. The unsupported-fact rate falls because law sentences, which the facts cannot support, give way to copied facts. These numbers are on dev, after selection, and were not taken to the blind slice.

\paragraph{Blind slice.} The freeze tags \texttt{prereg-v1} and \texttt{eval-freeze-v1} were created and pushed on 9 November before the blind slice was opened; the claims for c1457 are registered in Section~1.5 of the pre-registration (H-A1 to H-A5), with the deviations of its Section~8 (80 blind orders, one run per system, Sarvam only). The runs ended on 9 November at 10:54. The single gated scoring call then crashed in the claim checker on a draft sentence longer than its 512-token window, before writing any score. The scorer was fixed (a sentence that does not fit is checked in pieces and the least supported piece decides; every other sentence scores exactly as before, which a byte-identical rescoring of a scored dev arm confirms), the saved outputs were hashed, and the slice was scored once more (deviation D10). On the 80 blind orders c1457 has 46 wins, 69 ties and 5 losses against B0--B6, Full 29, 76 and 15 (Table~\ref{tab:arch_wlt}). H-A2 to H-A5 hold. c1457 beats Full on objection recall (0.52 to 0.73) and answerable coverage (0.36 to 0.61); it beats B4 and B5 on citation precision (0.71 against 0.02 and 0.10) and on the fabricated-citation rate (0.00 against 0.95 and 0.64); its unsupported-fact rate (0.89) is lower than every baseline's (0.94 to 1.00), a win against all seven; and it needs fewer store calls than B4. H-A1 is falsified. Besides the four cells declared structural before the opening (all four are losses on the blind slice), c1457 loses one more cell: the count of unsupported sentences against B5 (6.0 against 4.2 per order). B5 writes fewer sentences (4.4 against 7.0); on the rate c1457 beats B5. Head to head, c1457 has 8 wins, 13 ties and 3 losses against Full: it loses citation precision (0.85 to 0.71), citation recall and the unfinished-factor share, the price of drafting from the facts text and of listing unmet factors. The verdicts are the same with errored records kept as scored and with the two cases rerun under D7 dropped. The rule-only gold sensitivity was not run on the blind slice.
