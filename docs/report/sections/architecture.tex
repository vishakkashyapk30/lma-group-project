\section{Architecture search}
\label{sec:arch}

\paragraph{Why Full loses.} On the development runs Full does not beat every baseline (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Reading its outputs gives four causes. (1) The unsupported-fact rate checks every argument sentence against the facts of the case only; in Full's flagged sentences 55\% state a legal principle from a cited authority, 24\% say what the record does not state and 7\% carry a pseudonym label, so only about 9\% are real mismatches with the facts. (2) Munshi and Peshkar fill about 2.8 of the 13 dossier fields per case, and the rule-based stance picker never produces a custody-period, parity or trial-delay ground. (3) Abhiyojak's objections are checked against the material it was offered by an exact store label; on Sarvam Full kept 66 objections and discarded 230, and 263 of 320 dropped references named a paragraph that had in fact been offered. (4) Sentences that fail the check stay in the argument, marked, instead of becoming open issues.

\paragraph{Candidates.} Each fix is an opt-in switch with the default byte-identical (Section~\ref{sec:system}): X1 fills dossier gaps with rules over the raw facts; X2 splits case-fact sentences (near-verbatim spans of the record) from legal-basis sentences; X3 adds one Abhiyojak call on factors with no ground; X4 removes sentences that still fail after repair, and ``the record is silent'' sentences, from the argument and lists them as open issues for counsel; X5 lets Vakeel draft from the raw facts while the dossier still drives the planner and Gate~1; X6 searches Abhiyojak's stores a second time keyed on the facts; X7 resolves an objection's reference to offered material by its id; X8 replaces a case-fact sentence that paraphrases the record by the record sentence.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Sarvam-105B behind the pseudonymisation boundary. Search on the 30 dev orders t1dev-000..029 (single switches, then combinations); the 20 dev orders t1dev-030..049 are a holdout touched once, for the winner, Full and the baselines; the blind slice of the test set is run once from the freeze tag. Every arm of the final search table was rerun on one commit. A comparison is a win only when the paired-bootstrap interval at its Holm level excludes zero (10{,}000 resamples, seed 0; Holm at 0.05 over B0--B6 for each arm and metric). Quality metrics decide; cost is judged only against B4 and B5, the baselines of comparable capability; a metric a baseline cannot produce (objection precision when it raises no objection, the case-fact rate M6a when it writes no sentence markers) is listed as undefined, not won.

\gentable{arch_wlt}

\paragraph{Result.} The winner, c1457, is Full with X1, X4, X5 and X7 (Table~\ref{tab:arch_wlt}). On the 30 search orders it has 30 wins, 37 ties and 3 losses against B0--B6, where Full has 11, 49 and 10. Its objection recall rises from 0.36 to 1.00 (X7: the objections were there and were being discarded), answerable coverage from 0.38 to 0.61, coverage from 0.64 to 0.97, and the unsupported-fact rate falls from 0.99 to 0.90 (case-fact sentences alone: 0.84 to 0.47). It pays with citation precision (0.82 to 0.68, still a win over B4 and B5, which fabricate citations on 93\% and 64\% of orders) and 27\% more tokens. X2 failed on Sarvam (most grounds could not meet the verbatim rule and came out empty) and X8 added a loss for three more wins, so neither is in the winner. On the holdout the winner repeats: 22 wins, 26 ties, 3 losses, the same three cells.

\paragraph{The losses that remain are structural.} (1) Unfinished-factor share against B4 and B5: the winner plans 130 factors on the search orders and 47 end with a required slot missing (trial stage, witness examination, antecedents, community roots). A search of the facts text (not the gold) finds none of these 62 slots stated in the record, so ``missing'' is the true state; B4 and B5 have no required slots and score near zero by construction. Filling the slots would be fabrication; not planning those factors would drop the prosecution concerns counsel must prepare for. (2) Confident-wrong rate against B4: the metric counts a sentence as faithful only when the facts of the case support it, so a sentence stating a legal principle counts as wrong unless the court's order happens to repeat it; B4 emits 7.5\% of its grounds, a selected few sentences. (3) Over the pooled 50 orders, coverage against B6, a template that scores 1.00 by construction. None of these is chased, because closing them would change what the metric counts, not the brief.

\paragraph{Cost.} Table~\ref{tab:arch_cost}: the winner needs fewer store calls than B4 (109 against 186 per order on the search orders, a win) and as many tokens (a tie); it is slower than both and uses more tokens and store calls than B5. Against the one-call baselines and the template its cost is necessarily higher and is shown, not counted.

\gentable{arch_ablate}

\paragraph{Ablations of the winner.} Table~\ref{tab:arch_ablate} compares the winner with the winner minus one of its switches and with A1--A4 and A6--A9 applied on top of it (A1, A2, A8 and A9 drop the winner switch they cannot combine with, which the switch validator refuses). Removing X4 costs four wins and gains none; removing X1 or X5 changes nothing that the 30 orders can resolve (X5 alone lowered the unsupported-fact rate in the single-switch round, which X4 and X1 now largely cover). The remaining ablation rows are filled as their runs are scored.

\paragraph{Blind slice.} The freeze tags \texttt{prereg-v1} and \texttt{eval-freeze-v1} were created and pushed on 9 November before the blind slice was opened; the claims for c1457 are registered in Section~1.5 of the pre-registration (H-A1 to H-A5), with the deviations of its Section~8 (80 blind orders, one run per system, Sarvam only). The blind numbers are reported here once its single gated scoring has run.
